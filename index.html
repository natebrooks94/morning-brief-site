Diplomacy · Hormuz · Energy
StatusNo talks and no ceasefire deal. Trump dropped diplomacy Thursday and says he could restart the war at any time. Overnight came the first direct exchange in weeks: Iran says the US hit Sirik, and its Guards say they destroyed a US radar in Bahrain.

Iran says it destroyed a US radar in Bahrain after a strike on SirikNew

IRNA said the southern port of Sirik in Hormozgan was hit Thursday in a strike it called military in nature, with no damage detailsState media. Hours later, the Revolutionary Guards said their aerospace forces destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain in response to a strike on a communications tower there, according to FarsState media. AP and Reuters reported the claim. The material has no US confirmation or damage assessment.

If true, it would be Iran's first attack on US military assets in a Gulf country since early September. Iranian media also say the navy shot down a US MQ-1 Predator drone over HormuzState media. Guards commanders warned of a "horrifying and painful" response to any attack and said US bases are now within rangeState media.

Why it matters: A direct US–Iran exchange in a Gulf state is the clearest sign yet that the 'no war, no peace' standoff is cracking.

IAEA 'increasingly concerned' about Isfahan; clerics urge NPT exitNew

A confidential IAEA report seen by Reuters says the agency is "increasingly concerned" about an underground enrichment plant at Isfahan. Iran declared the plant last year but has never let inspectors in, and the agency doesn't know exactly where it is or whether it holds nuclear material. Iran told the agency that monitoring can't go ahead while the country is under threat.

Separately, the Assembly of Experts, the clerical body that picks the supreme leader, called for leaving the Non-Proliferation Treaty and revising nuclear doctrine. It is the first state institution to make that callOpposition-leaning.

Why it matters: An uninspected enrichment site plus official talk of quitting the NPT gives hawks in Washington and Jerusalem a new argument for striking.

IEA says oil is back in surplus, but fuel products stay tightDeveloping · Day 3

New today: the IEA's November report says the oil market swung into surplus in October thanks to strong Gulf exports, with a bigger build expected in 2027. It warns that supply of refined products such as diesel and jet fuel is still severely tight. Javier Blas estimates that crude prices delivered to refineries are down 35–40% since a mid-September peak. Freight rates on every route outside the Gulf are down 50–75%, though Gulf-to-Northeast Asia remains very expensive.

Europe: Goldman expects disruption to Gulf LNG to push Dutch TTF gas prices higher. Asia: refiners are buying Gulf crude again as flows rise and producers offer discounts. Asian airlines whose jet-fuel hedges are expiring face hedging at around $148 a barrel. GlobalData says the US is the biggest winner as the war redraws LNG trade.

Why it matters: Crude is easing, but tight diesel and jet fuel keep pressure on Europe and Asia and on US inflation.

The basic way to ask a price at any stall or shop. Use ¿Cuánto cuestan? (KWES-tahn) for more than one item, and ¿Cuánto es? at the till to ask what you owe.

Lugar del día

El Rastro (Sundays)

El Rastro is Madrid's oldest open-air flea market. It fills Calle de la Ribera de Curtidores and Plaza de Cascorro in La Latina every Sunday and on public holidays. You'll find antiques, vinyl, clothes, junk and treasure, and it's the perfect place to try out ¿Cuánto cuesta? Your next chance is this Sunday, Nov 15.

Hours
Sundays and holidays, 9 AM–3 PM. Stalls start packing up around 2:30 PM, and it's busiest 11 AM–2 PM.
Entry
Free; it's an open street market.
Metro
La Latina or Puerta de Toledo. Don't try to drive or park.
Tip
Bring cash, since many sellers don't take cards, and keep an eye on your phone in the crowds. Weekend road closures for races and protests may affect central traffic, and AEMET expects cloudy skies with light rain, so pack a light jacket.
